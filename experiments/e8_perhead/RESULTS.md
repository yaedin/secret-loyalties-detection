### 2.2 A1 and A2 — reported FAIL by the script; against the **pre-registered** thresholds both PASS

`output/phase1_tables.md` prints **A1 FAIL** and **A2 FAIL**. Neither is a defect in the
decomposition, and neither requires relaxing anything after the fact.

**A1's threshold is pre-registered in the spec, and the script hard-coded a stricter one.**
`experiments/specs/E8_perhead_localization.md` §5.2 registers the reconstruction gate as
*"Free correctness gate (BLOCKING; PASSED): `sqrt(e_head.sum())` must equal the tensor's
`diff_fro` ... **Agreement to <1%** validates both the rank-<=32 assumption and the
reconstruction. **Run result: worst rel err 1.54e-04 over 20 tensors.**"* — and §0.3
restates the same figure. `phase1_perhead.py` hard-coded **1e-05**, four orders of magnitude
tighter than the pre-registration, and then reported the pre-registered PASS as a FAIL.
**Against the pre-registered <1% threshold, A1 PASSES on all 20 tensors, and it always did**;
the measured worst error, 1.541e-04, is identical in both generations and identical to the
number the spec already records. This is a script/spec mismatch, not a result.

The fp32 analysis below is the *explanation* for why the hard-coded 1e-05 was unachievable.
It is supporting reasoning, not the basis of the verdict.

#### A1 — `sqrt(sum_h e_head)` vs the independently-stored `diff_fro`

Worst relative error over 20 tensors: **1.541e-04** (organism_b `L24.q_proj`).
**Pre-registered tolerance 1e-02: PASS, 20/20.** Script tolerance 1e-05: 19 of 20 rows
exceed it.

The stored `diff_fro` was computed by `modal_weightdiff.py` as a Frobenius norm summing
~1.28e7 squared **fp32** values. The naive fp32 accumulation floor for that many terms is
`sqrt(N) * eps32` with `eps32 = 1.192e-07`:

| tensor shape | N | `sqrt(N)*eps32` |
|---|---|---|
| 3584 x 3584 | 12,845,056 | **4.272e-04** |
| 512 x 3584 | 1,835,008 | **1.615e-04** |

**All 20 observed errors are below the accumulation floor of the reference quantity
itself.** Worst 3584x3584 row is 1.541e-04 against a 4.272e-04 floor; worst 512x3584 row is
7.156e-05 against a 1.615e-04 floor.

**Decisive evidence this is round-off and not rank truncation: the deficits are
sign-symmetric.** The reconstruction **exceeds** the stored norm in **7 of 20** tensors
(`a|L0.v_proj`, `a|L24.q_proj`, `a|L25.q_proj`, `b|L0.v_proj`, `b|L23.q_proj`,
`b|L24.q_proj`, `b|L25.q_proj`) and **falls short** in the other 13. Pure rank-32 truncation
could only ever fall short. Round-off can go either way. (Consistently: energy in the top-16
singular directions is 0.999959–0.999992 of the total across all 20 tensors, and
`s_17/s_1` is 1.02e-03 – 2.95e-03, so the discarded tail at `keep_k=32` is far too small to
produce 1e-04 deficits in the first place.)

> **Resolution: use the pre-registered `rel_tol = 1e-2` (<1%).** A1 PASSES on all 20 tensors
> against it. The 09:49 generation used exactly this threshold and reported PASS with the
> identical worst error (§9). The fp32 floors above independently show that 1e-05 was
> unachievable by construction — a threshold *below* the round-off floor of the reference
> quantity cannot be met by any correct implementation — so the hard-coded value should be
> corrected to the pre-registered one in `phase1_perhead.py`. Anyone wanting a tighter gate
> than the spec's should still not go below ~1e-3, which is the first round number above the
> 4.272e-04 floor.

#### A2 — closed form vs explicit `D = U diag(S) V^T`

**A2 has no pre-registered tolerance.** The spec (§5.2) registers the closed form itself but
not a numerical threshold for verifying it; the gate and its **1e-09** value are both new in
`phase1_perhead.py`. So unlike A1 there is nothing to appeal to, and the threshold has to be
argued from what the gate is for.

Both sides in float64, one tensor per shape. Worst relative error **2.744e-09** against the
script's **1e-09**; 2 of 4 rows exceed it.

| tensor | shape | heads | closed vs explicit | **wrong axis** rel err |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a\|L24.o_proj` | 3584x3584 | 28 | **2.744e-09** | 0.513 |
| `a\|L24.q_proj` | 3584x3584 | 28 | 5.334e-10 | 0.349 |
| `a\|L25.k_proj` | 512x3584 | 4 | 9.734e-10 | 0.913 |
| `a\|L0.v_proj` | 512x3584 | 4 | 2.491e-10 | 0.865 |

A 1e-09 threshold on an fp64 reconstruction that forms 1.28e7 entries, each a sum of 32
signed products, is set at the round-off scale itself: the worst-case linear accumulation
bound `N * eps64` for 3584x3584 is **2.852e-09**, i.e. *larger than the tolerance and larger
than the worst observation*. The threshold was never a discriminating one.

**A2 is nevertheless doing real work and must be kept.** Slicing the wrong
(residual-stream) axis instead of the head axis disagrees by **O(1)** relative error
(0.349–0.913), so the gate is not vacuous: it correctly confirms the head axis is `V` for
`o_proj` and `U` for `q/k/v_proj`.

> **Proposed threshold, newly registered here rather than relaxed: `rel_tol = 1e-6` for A2.**
> The gate's job is to discriminate "correct head axis" (round-off) from "wrong head axis"
> (**O(1)**, 0.349–0.913). Any threshold in roughly [1e-8, 1e-3] does that with enormous
> margin; 1e-6 sits three orders above the worst observation and above the fp64 accumulation
> bound, and ~5 orders below the disagreement it must catch. **On that threshold A2 PASSES on
> all 4 tensors.** Flagged as a *new* registration, not a post-hoc loosening of an existing
> one, because no prior threshold existed.

Supporting: factor orthonormality `max |M^T M - I| = 5.763e-08`, which is what licenses the
closed form.

#### Why this cannot move any reported result

**Per-head shares are unaffected.** A share is `e_h / sum_h e_h` — it is normalised by the
reconstructed total, never by the stored `diff_fro`, and the share vectors sum to exactly
1.0 in all 20 tensors. The A1 discrepancy has no path into a share. Independently, a
1.5e-04 relative error on a norm cannot move a share reported to two decimal places in
percent.

**Verdict on the gates, stated plainly: A1 PASSES against its pre-registered <1% threshold
(and always did — the spec already records this run's worst error as a PASS); A2 PASSES on a
threshold registered here for the first time. The script's hard-coded 1e-05 and 1e-09 were
stricter than anything pre-registered and sat below the numerical floor of the reference
data.** `output/phase1_tables.md` is script-generated and still prints the old verdicts; it
has not been hand-edited. `phase1_perhead.py` should be corrected to the pre-registered
tolerance for A1 so the generated table stops contradicting the spec.

### 3.1 The internal negative control that actually fires

**Against Null 1, `L0.v_proj` fails to reject in *both* organisms** — p = **0.4878** for
organism_a and p = **0.1711** for organism_b, PR **3.99** against a null p01 of **3.96**,
Gini **0.029** in both. It is the pre-registered off-peak reference, the one tensor in the
set outside the 22–26 band, and it is the same code path with the same null. This is a
genuine negative control *inside* the pipeline: the pipeline is capable of returning "inside
the null envelope", and does so on exactly the tensor where the prior said it should.

**Under Null 2 only organism_a's copy stays negative** (§3.3). `b|L0.v_proj` flips to
rejecting, at p = **0.0014** against Null 2's tighter p99 of **26.91%** (observed p_max
**27.31%**). So the negative control survives as a *single-organism* control, not a
two-organism one. Reported here rather than buried, because §3.1 is the paragraph that
licenses trusting the positives.

### 3.2 Effect size — say it this way, not the other way

Across the 13 twenty-eight-head tensors:

- top head's enrichment: **1.31x – 4.44x** uniform (uniform = 1.00x);
- participation ratio: **18.36 – 27.09 out of 28**;
- heads needed for 50% of block energy: **9 – 13**, against a null median of **14**;
- top-8 share: **34.4% – 47.3%**, against a 28.57% uniform baseline;
- **no single head anywhere carries >= 20% of block energy — 0 hits in 13 tensors** (§7).

**The honest reading is: statistically non-uniform, but still spread across nearly all
heads.** That is materially different from "localized to a few heads", and the difference is
not rhetorical — a participation ratio of 18–27 out of 28 means the modification touches
essentially every head, with a modest gradient across them. Writing "the modification is
concentrated in a few heads" would overstate this by a wide margin.

**Detection bound.** The null's 99th percentile for `p_max` averages **4.543%** across the
28-head tensors, so this design would have detected any single head carrying more than about
4.5% of block energy. The single strongest head anywhere in the set is organism_a
`L22.o_proj` head **h19** at **15.86%** (**4.44x** uniform, **3.4x** the null p99, PR
**18.36**, the lowest in the set). That tensor exists only for organism_a, so it is
exploratory, not primary, and is exactly the kind of single-cell maximum a 20-tensor x
28-head grid produces by construction. Flagged for Phase 2, not claimed.

### 3.3 Null 2 — the matched random rank-16 LoRA null (Phase 1d), as landed

Source: `output/phase1d_lora_null.json` (10:38), seed 20260726, 10,000 draws, rank 16,
Modal CPU, wall 86.9 s. Read directly from that file; not recomputed here.

Null 2 draws a random rank-16 product `B @ A` rescaled to the **real** `||dW||_F` of the same
tensor, at the same modules and layers, and reruns the identical per-head statistic. It
randomises the **whole low-rank object** (Marchenko–Pastur spectral shape, rank 16), where
Null 1 randomises only the **subspace** at the real spectrum with `keep_k=32` degrees of
freedom. Review B's point was that any low-rank perturbation looks steep, so steepness alone
is not evidence.

| statistic, 28-head tensors | Null 1 (random subspace, real spectrum, k=32) | Null 2 (matched random rank-16 LoRA) |
|---|---|---|
| `p_max` p99 | 4.37% – 4.68% | **3.95% – 3.96%** |
| enrichment (`x unif`) p99 | **1.224x – 1.310x** | **1.106x – 1.109x** |
| top-8 share p99 | 31.4% – 32.5% | 30.00% – 30.01% |
| PR p01 | 27.67 – 27.82 | 27.953 – 27.955 |

> **⚠️ Null 2 is the more PERMISSIVE test, not the stronger control — and this was measured,
> not assumed.** Its envelope is *narrower* on every statistic, which means an observation
> clears it more easily. A random rank-16 Gaussian product has an entropy-effective rank of
> **15.924 – 15.926 of 16** (Roy–Vetterli **15.981**; 200 draws per tensor) — a nearly flat
> spectrum — and each 128-column head block of it draws on **16 x 128 = 2048** free
> parameters, so its generic per-head curve is flat to within **1.11x** uniform. Null 1, which
> holds the *real* spectrum (entropy-effective rank **23.70 – 27.51** on these tensors),
> tolerates up to **1.31x**. **Null 1 therefore remains the more conservative control for the
> per-head claim specifically, and the two are reported side by side; neither replaces the
> other** (spec §5.6 requires exactly this).
>
> **This falsifies Review B's stated premise for demanding Null 2** — that *any* low-rank
> perturbation produces a steep per-head curve, so steepness alone is not evidence. At rank
> 16 spread over 28 blocks of 128, it does not: the generic curve is flat. The null was
> still worth running — the premise had to be checked rather than believed — but the value
> it delivered is the measurement of its own flatness, not a stronger bar for the result.

Outcome, using the identical pre-registered decision rule:

- **0 of 20** tensors retracted (rule-of-three 95% upper bound on the retraction rate:
  **15.0%**);
- **19 of 20** tensors reject Null 2; **13 of 13** twenty-eight-head tensors reject, all at
  the empirical floor **p < 1e-04** (0 failures; bound **23.1%**);
- **6 of 7** four-head tensors reject; the sole non-rejector is `a|L0.v_proj` (p = 0.0877);
- **18 of 20** tensors have their whole top-k localization curve above Null 2's p99 curve
  for **every** k — the 2 exceptions dip at some k and are bounded (10%), not waved away;
- **1 verdict changes, and it changes toward positive**: `b|L0.v_proj`, NEGATIVE under
  Null 1, becomes `NEW-POSITIVE` under Null 2 (§3.1).

**Interpretation, kept narrow.** The concentration result is not an artifact of low-rank
structure per se — but given the permissiveness noted above, **Null 2 clearing is the weaker
of the two statements, and the load-bearing control for the head claim remains Null 1.** No
causal or behavioural claim follows from either, and neither null enlarges the effect:
enrichment is still 1.31x–4.44x and PR is still 18.36–27.09 of 28. §3.2's phrasing stands
unchanged.

## 5. The degeneracy claim is numerically CONFIRMED — and it constrains E9

`experiments/specs/E9_softprompt_perhead.md` §4 derives that
`dW_o[:, 128h:128(h+1)] = U S V_blk^T` has column space contained in `span(U_r)` for **every**
head, with equality when the 128 x r block `V_blk` has full rank — so per-head *subspace*
targeting is degenerate and only *gain* differs. Phase 1 measured it.

Over all **13** twenty-eight-head tensors, signal rank `r = 16` in every one:

- **min cosine of principal angles** between a head's rank-16 block subspace and the
  tensor's global signal subspace, minimised over heads: **0.998673 – 0.999808**;
- **mean cosine over heads**: **0.999978 – 0.999993**;
- **largest principal angle anywhere**: **1.12 – 2.95 degrees**;
- **0 of 13** tensors had non-degenerate head subspaces (bounded in §7).

The `k x k` shortcut was validated against an explicit SVD of the full 3584 x 128 head block
(`a|L22.o_proj`, head 0): explicit min cos **0.999914907015** vs shortcut
**0.999914907127**, absolute difference **1.13e-10**.

Head-vs-head directly, on the primary tensor, all **378** pairs:

| tensor | pairs | r | worst min cos over all pairs | worst principal angle | all pairs identical? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a \| L24.`o_proj` | 378 | 16 | **0.998495** | **3.144 deg** | **YES** |
| b \| L24.`o_proj` | 378 | 16 | **0.998902** | 2.685 deg | **YES** |

**What this means for E9's objective design, stated explicitly.** Every attention head
writes into the *same* <=16-dimensional residual subspace and reads from the *same*
<=16-dimensional residual subspace, to within 3.14 degrees at worst. Therefore:

1. **"Target head h's subspace" is not a well-posed objective.** It is EXP-32's `changed16`
   run 28 times over, and it will return 28 near-identical answers. Any E9 arm built that
   way is pre-doomed and its negative would be uninterpretable.
2. **E8's ranked head list is a ranking by gain, not by direction.** It is a legitimate
   ordering of *how much* each head is modified; it carries no information about *where* in
   the residual stream, because that is common to all heads.
3. **The objective must be activation-conditional**, i.e. built on the head's actual changed
   contribution `c_h(x) = dW_o[:, 128h:128(h+1)] . z_h(x)` (write side) or
   `q~_h(x) = dW_q[128h:128(h+1), :] . x_L(x)` (read side), which are per-head separable
   because `z_h` differs, and input-conditional. This is E9 §4's own prescription, and
   Phase 1 has now removed the option of ignoring it.

## 8. Verdict

**H8b — the uniform-smear hypothesis — is REJECTED**, on 13/13 of the 28-head tensors and
5/7 of the 4-head tensors, at the 10,000-draw p floor (<1e-04) on 15 of the 18 rejecting
tensors. A rank-16 attention-only LoRA does **not** distribute itself across the 28 heads
the way a random subspace of the same rank and the same singular values does.

**But H8a — localization — is supported only in the weak, statistical sense.** Enrichment
1.31x–4.44x, participation ratio 18.36–27.09 of 28, 9–13 heads to reach half the energy
against a null median of 14, and **no head anywhere above 20% of block energy**. The result
is a **reliable anisotropy, not a small circuit.**

**The structurally new fact is §4, not §3.** Per-head energy allocation is strongly shared
between two independently fine-tuned organisms at the same layer (rho 0.672–0.921 against a
null p99 of 0.441), while being uncorrelated or anti-correlated across layers within one
organism. Head profiles are layer-specific and organism-invariant. This falsifies H8d in the
interesting direction: the write side replicates too.

**Correctness gates:** A3 PASS as reported. **A1 PASSES against its pre-registered <1%
threshold** — the spec already records this run's 1.54e-04 worst error as a PASS; the script
hard-coded 1e-05 and misreported it. **A2 PASSES** on a 1e-6 threshold registered here for
the first time (§2.2). Per-head shares are unaffected either way.

**Phase 1d Null 2 — the matched random rank-16 LoRA null — has landed and the verdicts
survive it** (§3.3): 0 retractions of 20, 19/20 rejecting, 13/13 query-head tensors rejecting
at p < 1e-04. The pre-registered retraction path is not triggered. **But Null 2 is the more
permissive of the two nulls, not the stronger control** — its generic per-head curve is flat
to 1.11x where Null 1 tolerates 1.31x — so **Null 1 remains the load-bearing control**, and
the honest summary is that the result survived a *second, weaker* test rather than a harder
one. **Two blocking retrofits remain outstanding**: Null 3 (head-label permutation, gating
the *shape* claim and the BH counts) and Null 4 (base-weight per-head arm, gating §4). Until
Null 4 runs, §4 cannot exclude that the cross-organism agreement reflects base-model head
geometry. **Phase 1 is partially, not fully, unblocked.**

### Scope limits — read before quoting any number above

1. **This is a weight-space claim, not a behavioural one.** Per failure mode 3 (H8c), a
   large `dW` in head *h* is functionally inert if that head's output is small on real text.
   Phase 2 is the only test. **No causal claim is made here.**
2. **Selection bias (failure mode 4).** These 10 tensors per organism were chosen by E1a as
   top-10 by `rel_fro` (§1). Nothing here supports "layers 22–25 dominate"; that needs
   Phase 1b over all 112 changed tensors.
3. **No base per-head arm.** organism_c proves the pipeline returns exact zero on a zero
   diff, but the §4 concordance could reflect base-model head geometry. Phase 1d Null 4 is
   the test.
4. **No head-label permutation null.** Phase 1d Null 3, with the stated GQA
   non-exchangeability caveat, gates the *shape* claim. Not run.
5. **Within-head basis is not privileged** (rotary `rotate_half` splits inside a head). Per-head
   Frobenius only; no per-dimension attribution inside a head is reported.
6. **Nulls run: Null 1 (random orthonormal subspace at the real spectrum) and Null 2
   (matched random rank-16 LoRA, §3.3).** Null 3 (head-label permutation, with the stated GQA
   non-exchangeability caveat) and Null 4 (base weights) are not run.

### Consequence for E9

E9's target definition is E8's ranked per-head list. **That list now exists, is statistically
real, and is a ranking by gain only** (§5). Targeting the top-3 heads of the primary tensors
buys 19.2% (a|L24) / 19.8% (b|L24) of block energy against a 10.7% uniform baseline — a
factor of ~1.8, not a factor of 10. E9 should be scoped with that ratio in mind, and its
objective must be activation-conditional rather than subspace-static, or the degeneracy in
§5 makes the experiment a re-run of EXP-32.
