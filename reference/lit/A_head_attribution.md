## 0. Bottom line for E8 — what to compute, in priority order

The single most important finding of this review, stated bluntly:

> **As specified, E8 Phases 1 and 2 contain zero causal measurements.** Per-head
> Frobenius energy of ΔW (Phase 1) and per-head ‖Δz_h‖ on a corpus (Phase 2) are both
> *magnitude* statistics. In the vocabulary the field has used since at least Vig et al.
> 2020 **[V]**, these are **correlational**. They can license a *screening* claim
> ("these heads are where we should look") and they can license a **negative** claim
> (if everything is inside the null envelope, nothing is concentrated). They cannot
> license a *localization* claim ("this head carries the loyalty edit"). Every
> localization result the field currently accepts — IOI, causal tracing, retrieval
> heads, copy suppression — is built on an intervention, not a norm.

Priority-ordered actions:

1. **Keep Phase 1 exactly as specced, but relabel its output.** Per-head ‖ΔW‖ with a
   10k-draw matched random-subspace null is a legitimate, cheap **screen** and a
   legitimate **negative test**. Report it as "weight-side energy distribution", never as
   "localization". Its own §8.3 (H8c) already says this; make it the headline framing,
   not a caveat.
2. **Add a causal Phase 3 before writing any positive claim.** The cheapest valid causal
   primitive available to you is **weight-space head grafting**, and it is nearly free
   because you already hold the exact ΔW blocks in factored form:
   - **Necessity (knockout):** take the organism, restore base weights in head *h*'s
     `o_proj` column block (equivalently, zero `ΔW_o[:, 128h:128(h+1)]`), measure the
     behavioural metric. Sweep *h*.
   - **Sufficiency (graft):** take the **base** model, add organism `ΔW_o` for head *h*
     only, measure. Sweep *h*.
   These are the weight-space analogues of resample-ablation and activation patching, and
   they are the necessity/sufficiency pair the field expects (Section 4). The base model
   *is* your resample distribution — you are not going off-distribution the way zero-
   ablation does (Section 2.1).
3. **Fix a behavioural metric with a real null before you sweep.** You have exactly one
   clean task metric: the **refusal rate on the protective-ask battery (base 98.7% vs
   organisms 0.7%, bf16)**. Convert it to a continuous, low-variance score — a
   **first-token logit difference** between a refusal continuation and a compliance
   continuation, teacher-forced, averaged over the 40-prompt EXTREME set. Discrete
   refusal-rate over 40 prompts is far too noisy for a 28-way sweep; logit-diff is the
   metric activation-patching best-practice work converged on (Zhang & Nanda 2024 **[V]**).
   Report the *normalised* recovery fraction: `(score_intervened − score_base) /
   (score_organism − score_base)`, so 0 = base-like, 1 = full organism behaviour.
4. **Run a full-model AtP/attribution-patching sweep to make the 784-block grid cheap.**
   One forward + one backward pass on the organism gives you a linear estimate of every
   head's effect on your metric simultaneously (Nanda 2023 **[lab-blog/forum, V]**; Syed,
   Rager & Conmy **[V]**; Kramár et al. AtP* **[V]**). Use it to *rank*, then verify the
   top-k and a matched random-k with true grafting. **Do not report AtP numbers as
   results** — AtP* exists specifically because plain attribution patching has
   documented false-negative failure modes **[V]**.
5. **Nulls, per measurement.** (a) *Random-head null* — the identical statistic on k
   uniformly-sampled heads, same k as your "top" set. (b) *Structural null* —
   `organism_c` / `base_dup`, whose ΔW is identically zero; any nonzero output is a bug.
   (c) *Off-peak layer null* — layers 0 and 26, already in D3. (d) *Random head-sized
   subspace* — a 128-dim block of a random rotation of the concat-head space, already in
   D5. All four are already half-specced; the missing one is (a) applied to the *causal*
   sweep, not just the magnitude sweep.
6. **Report a Pareto/faithfulness curve, not a top-1.** The accepted presentation is
   *metric recovered vs number of heads ablated*, with the importance-ordered curve
   plotted against a **random-order** curve and ideally a **reverse-order** curve. If the
   importance curve is not visibly separated from random order, you have no localization
   — and that is the exact shape of the result Michel et al. **[V]** and Prasanna et al.
   **[V]** got in the pruning literature (Section 3).
7. **Pre-commit the primary test and BH-correct it; label everything else exploratory.**
   D9 already does this (o_proj @ L24, L25, 28 heads, BH). Good. Add: report the
   **minimum detectable effect at 80% power** for the negative case, because H8b is the
   honest prior and a bounded null is a publishable result while an unbounded one is not.
8. **Handle GQA honestly by splitting the report in two.** Query-head-indexed quantities
   (`q_proj` rows, `o_proj` columns, per-head attention outputs) are **28-way and clean**.
   KV-indexed quantities (`k_proj`, `v_proj`) are **4-way**, and ablating a KV head is a
   *group* intervention hitting 7 query heads at once. Never put them in the same ranking.
   The literature on this is genuinely thin (Section 6) — say so in RESULTS.md.
9. **Expect and instrument for self-repair.** Single-head ablation in real LMs is
   partially compensated by downstream components (Rushing & Nanda **[V]**). A near-zero
   knockout effect is therefore *not* proof of irrelevance. Mitigate by reporting the
   *direct* effect (the head's own write into the residual stream, which Phase 2's
   `ΔW_o a_h` term already isolates) alongside the *total* effect from grafting.
10. **A specific structural warning that is unique to your setting and that I could not
    find addressed anywhere in the literature.** For a LoRA delta `ΔW = B A` with
    `B ∈ ℝ^{3584×16}`, `A ∈ ℝ^{16×3584}`, the `o_proj` column block for head *h* is
    `B · A[:, 128h:128(h+1)]`. So **the entire per-head profile of `o_proj` is a property
    of the 16×3584 factor `A` alone** — the left factor `B` is shared by every head.
    Symmetrically, the `q_proj` per-head row profile is a property of `B` alone. This
    means the head-to-head variation you will measure in Phase 1 is a *low-rank
    projection artefact by construction*, and a rank-16 object spread over 28 blocks of
    128 columns has very limited freedom to look uniform even under a null. Your matched
    random-**rank-16**-subspace null (D5) is exactly the right control for this, which is
    to your credit — but the interpretation must be stated: **you are testing whether the
    LoRA's right factor is head-aligned, not whether the behaviour is head-localized.**
    Those are different claims. (Derived here from the architecture; not a citation.)

## 1. Correlational vs causal: what a magnitude claim licenses

### 1.1 The field's founding negative result

The modern insistence on causal evidence in attention analysis traces to a concrete
embarrassment: attention *weights* were widely read as explanations, and then shown not
to be. Jain & Wallace, *Attention is not Explanation* **[peer-reviewed, NAACL 2019,
arXiv:1902.10186] [V]** found learned attention weights "frequently uncorrelated with
gradient-based measures of feature importance," and constructed alternative attention
distributions yielding identical predictions. Wiegreffe & Pinter, *Attention is not not
Explanation* **[peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2019, arXiv:1908.04626] [V]** pushed back — the
claim depends on your definition of explanation, and they proposed uniform-weight
baselines, multi-seed variance calibration, and a frozen-weights diagnostic. The lasting
methodological residue of that exchange is not who won; it is that **a quantity computed
from the forward pass, without intervening on it, is a hypothesis generator, and the
field now expects a baseline and a seed-variance estimate attached to it.**

The generalisation of that lesson to internal quantities is the *interpretability
illusion* literature. Bolukbasi et al., *An Interpretability Illusion for BERT*
**[preprint, arXiv:2104.07143] [V]** show that a neuron can spuriously appear to encode a
single simple concept while encoding something far more complex, and attribute this
partly to dataset narrowness — their methodological demand is validation across multiple
datasets. Makelov, Lange & Nanda, *Is This the Subspace You Are Looking For?*
**[peer-reviewed workshop, NeurIPS 2023 ATTRIB, arXiv:2311.17030] [V]** show the harder
version: even a *successful causal intervention* on a subspace can be illusory, because
the effect may be achieved by activating a dormant parallel pathway that is causally
disconnected from the output on real inputs. This is directly relevant to you: your
edit lives in a 16-dimensional subspace, and subspace interventions are exactly where
this illusion was demonstrated.

### 1.2 What a per-head norm actually licenses

Given a per-head statistic *S(h)* computed without intervention (Frobenius energy of
ΔW_h; ‖Δz_h‖ on a corpus; attention-pattern JS divergence):

**Licensed:**
- *Screening.* "Heads with large *S* are where an intervention study should start."
- *Distributional / negative claims.* "The distribution of *S* over heads is
  statistically indistinguishable from a matched random-subspace null, therefore the
  edit is not concentrated in any head **in this basis, by this measure**." This is the
  strongest thing Phase 1 can produce and it is genuinely valuable — see Section 8.
- *Architectural description.* "The change is confined to attention projections at layers
  22–25" — a statement about *where parameters moved*, which is a fact about the training
  run, not about the computation.

**Not licensed:**
- *Necessity.* Large *S(h)* says nothing about whether removing *h* changes behaviour.
  A head whose weights changed a lot but whose activations `a_h` are near zero on real
  text contributes nothing. E8's own H8c states this; the literature agrees emphatically.
- *Sufficiency.* Even less. Nothing about a norm implies head *h* alone reproduces the
  behaviour.
- *Any transfer to editing/repair.* Hase et al., *Does Localization Inform Editing?*
  **[peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2023 spotlight, arXiv:2301.04213] [V]** is the sharpest
  available warning. They found that localization from Causal Tracing — a *causal*
  method, note, far stronger than a norm — "do not provide any insight into which model
  MLP layer would be best to edit." If a causal localization does not tell you where to
  edit, a Frobenius norm certainly does not tell you where to ablate.
- *Rank ordering as ground truth.* Michel et al. **[V]** found positive but only
  moderate correlation (>0.5) between head-removal effects measured on two different
  test sets. Head importance is dataset-conditional even when measured causally.

### 1.3 Why the field is strict about this specifically for heads

Because the residual stream makes per-head decomposition *look* free. Elhage et al.,
*A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits* **[lab-blog, Anthropic /
Transformer Circuits Thread, 2021-12-22] [V]** establishes that "attention heads can be
understood as independent operations, each outputting a result which is added into the
residual stream," and that the concat-and-multiply formulation is mathematically
equivalent to the independent-additive one. This is what makes `o_proj` column blocks
meaningful and what makes E8's §3 "unifying rule" correct. But additivity of *outputs*
does not imply independence of *function*: heads read each other's writes through later
layers, so a per-head decomposition of a forward pass is a decomposition of the
*bookkeeping*, not of the causal structure. That gap is the whole reason interventions
exist.

## 2. The per-head intervention toolkit

### 2.1 Ablation: zero vs mean vs resample vs optimal

**Zero-ablation** sets a component's output to 0. It is the oldest and the most
criticised. The critique is distributional: a transformer's components were never
trained to receive a zero-vector write, so downstream components are pushed to
activations unreachable from any input, and the resulting damage measures "how confused
the model is by an impossible internal state," not "how much this head contributed." The
causal scrubbing writeup (Chan et al., Redwood Research, *Causal Scrubbing: a method for
rigorously testing interpretability hypotheses*, AI Alignment Forum, Dec 2022)
**[forum, V — post exists at alignmentforum.org/posts/JvZhhzycHu2Yd57RN]** frames its
whole contribution against this: unlike standard zero and mean ablations, it ablates by
**resampling activations from other inputs**, keeping the model on-distribution. Note
Michel et al. **[V]** *do* zero-ablate (they set a per-head mask variable ξ_h = 0) —
zero-ablation is not invalid, it is just the weakest baseline, and 2019-era pruning work
predates the critique.

**Mean-ablation** replaces the output with its mean over a reference distribution. Better:
the component now writes "typical" information, so downstream components stay closer to
distribution. Its residual problem is that a mean is not a member of the distribution
when the distribution is multi-modal, and it silently *preserves* whatever information is
constant across the reference set. **This is a live hazard for you specifically:** ~62%
of your activation diff is a single constant vector `d̄`. Mean-ablating over a
mixed-model reference would preserve `d̄`; mean-ablating over an organism-only reference
would remove it wholesale. Choose the reference set deliberately and state it.

**Resample / activation-patching ablation** replaces the component's activation with its
value on a *different, real* input. This is the current default in circuit work and the
core of causal scrubbing **[forum, V]** and of ACDC (Conmy et al., *Towards Automated
Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability*, **[peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2023
Spotlight, arXiv:2304.14997] [V]**). Its cost is that it requires a paired-prompt design
— a "clean" and a "corrupted" prompt differing minimally in the property of interest.

**Optimal ablation** (Li & Janson, *Optimal ablation for interpretability*, **[preprint,
arXiv:2409.09951] [V]**) replaces the component's output with the constant that minimises
the loss, arguing theoretical and empirical advantages over zero/mean/resample for
circuit discovery, factual-recall localization and latent prediction. Worth knowing;
not yet the field default.

**The result you must internalise before choosing:** Miller, Chughtai & Saunders,
*Transformer Circuit Faithfulness Metrics are not Robust* **[preprint,
arXiv:2407.08734] [V]** find existing faithfulness methods "highly sensitive to seemingly
insignificant changes in the ablation methodology," concluding that faithfulness scores
"reflect both the methodological choices of researchers as well as the actual components
of the circuit." **Practical consequence for E8: run your causal sweep under at least two
ablation regimes and report both.** For you the natural pair is (i) restore-base-weights
(a principled resample: the ablated state is a real, trained model's weights) and
(ii) zero the ΔW block *only*, which for `o_proj` is the same thing — so your second arm
should instead be mean- or resample-ablation of the head's *activation* `a_h`, giving an
independent methodological axis.

**And a second one:** Rushing & Nanda, *Explorations of Self-Repair in Language Models*
**[preprint, arXiv:2402.15390] [V]** show that when a head is ablated, later components
change behaviour to compensate; recovery is imperfect and noisy, sometimes
overcorrecting, and is driven partly by LayerNorm rescaling and by sparse
"Anti-Erasure" neurons. Their own stated implication is that single-head ablation studies
can mislead about component importance. **For a 28-way knockout sweep, self-repair is the
main reason to expect uniformly small effects even if localization is real.**

### 2.2 Activation patching / causal tracing

Meng et al., *Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT* (ROME) **[peer-reviewed,
NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2202.05262] [V]** is the canonical causal-tracing reference: corrupt
the input so the model fails, then restore activations from the clean run at specific
layers and positions, identifying "which computations are necessary for factual recall."
Vig et al., *Causal Mediation Analysis for Interpreting Neural NLP: The Case of Gender
Bias* **[preprint, arXiv:2004.12265] [V]** supplies the formal vocabulary — total,
direct and indirect effect — and, importantly for you, applies it **at the level of
individual neurons and attention heads**, finding effects sparse and concentrated with
synergistic interactions between components. If you want a principled decomposition of
"head h's own contribution" vs "head h's contribution routed through later components,"
causal mediation is the framework, and it maps almost exactly onto Phase 2 §6.2's
`ΔW_o a_h` vs `W_o^base Δa_h` split.

Methodology: Zhang & Nanda, *Towards Best Practices of Activation Patching in Language
Models: Metrics and Methods* **[peer-reviewed, ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.16042] [V]** is the
one to actually read before writing code. Their headline is that these hyperparameters —
the evaluation metric (logit difference, probability, KL divergence) and the corruption
method — are *not* interchangeable and "varying these hyperparameters could lead to
disparate interpretability results." Heimersheim & Nanda, *How to use and interpret
activation patching* **[preprint, arXiv:2404.15255] [V]** is the tutorial companion.

**Direction matters and is routinely confused.** "Denoising" (patch clean activations
into a corrupted run) tests *sufficiency* of the patched component. "Noising" (patch
corrupted into clean) tests *necessity*. They can give different answers; report which
you ran. In your weight-space translation: grafting organism-ΔW into base = denoising =
sufficiency; restoring base weights inside the organism = noising = necessity.

### 2.3 Path patching

Goldowsky-Dill et al., *Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching* **[preprint,
arXiv:2304.05969] [V]** generalise patching from *nodes* to *paths*: it lets you
"express and quantitatively test a natural class of hypotheses expressing that behaviors
are localized to a set of paths," i.e. to particular edges in the computational graph.
Wang et al.'s IOI circuit **[preprint, arXiv:2211.00593] [V]** is the flagship
application — 26 attention heads sorted into 7 functional groups, evaluated on
faithfulness, completeness and minimality.

**My assessment for E8: path patching is out of scope and you should say so explicitly
rather than omitting it.** Path patching answers "does head A influence head B's query
input specifically?" You do not have a circuit hypothesis of that shape; you have a
diffuse always-on weight delta. Node-level necessity/sufficiency is the right resolution
for a first pass. Reserve path patching for a follow-up if — and only if — Phase 3 finds
a small set of heads that matters.

### 2.4 Attribution patching and successors

Attribution patching takes a first-order Taylor expansion of the patching effect,
replacing a per-component sweep with two forward passes and one backward pass. Origin:
Nanda, *Attribution Patching: Activation Patching At Industrial Scale*
**[lab-blog/forum, neelnanda.io + LessWrong, March 2023] [V]** — his framing is that in
three passes you could patch all of GPT-3's neurons. Formalised and benchmarked by Syed,
Rager & Conmy, *Attribution Patching Outperforms Automated Circuit Discovery*
**[preprint, arXiv:2310.10348] [V]**, who apply "a linear approximation to activation
patching to estimate the importance of each edge in the computational subgraph" at a cost
of "just two forward passes and a backward pass," and report greater circuit-recovery AUC
than alternatives when averaged over tasks.

**Where the approximation breaks — this is the part to design around.** Kramár, Lieberum,
Shah & Nanda, *AtP\*: An efficient and scalable method for localizing LLM behaviour to
components* **[preprint, Google DeepMind, arXiv:2403.00745] [V]** identify "two classes
of failure modes of AtP which lead to significant false negatives," fix them while
keeping scalability, and — the part that matters for null discipline — **provide a method
to bound the probability of remaining false negatives**. If you run an AtP sweep, run
AtP\* and report the false-negative bound; a bounded sweep converts "we found nothing" from
an absence into a measurement. Hanna, Pezzelle & Belinkov, *Have Faith in Faithfulness*
**[preprint, arXiv:2403.17806] [V]** add an orthogonal warning: EAP-derived circuits can
have high *node overlap* with causally-derived circuits while being *less faithful*, and
they argue "faithfulness, not overlap, is what should be measured." Translation: do not
validate your AtP ranking by checking it agrees with your Frobenius ranking. Agreement
between two cheap proxies is not evidence.

Ancestry note, useful because it reframes Phase 1: Michel et al. **[V]** already used a
gradient-based head-importance proxy in 2019, defined (per the ar5iv full text) as
`I_h = E |Att_h(x)ᵀ ∂ℒ(x)/∂Att_h(x)|` — literally activation-times-gradient on the head
output. **This is the same functional form as attribution patching against a
zero-ablation baseline.** So the "cheap all-heads sweep" is a seven-year-old idea with a
modern error analysis; you get to use the modern version for free.

### 2.5 Direct logit attribution

DLA exploits the linearity of the residual stream (Elhage et al. **[lab-blog, V]**): each
component's write can be projected directly onto the unembedding to get its direct
contribution to a logit. It is essentially free and it is the natural companion to your
`ΔW_o a_h` term.

Its documented failure mode is erasure. Janiak, Rager, Dao & Lau, *An Adversarial Example
for Direct Logit Attribution: Memory Management in GELU-4L* **[peer-reviewed workshop,
BlackboxNLP 2024, arXiv:2310.07325] [V]** show empirically that later heads and MLPs
actively erase earlier components' residual-stream writes, and that DLA "does not account
for erasure," so attribution scores can be deceptive. Copy suppression (McDougall, Conmy,
Rushing, McGrath & Nanda, **[preprint, arXiv:2310.04625] [V]**) is a named instance of the
same phenomenon at head level.

**For E8:** DLA is worth computing as a descriptive layer, but with 28 layers and the
edit sitting at layers 22–25, there are still 2–5 layers of potential erasure downstream.
Do not make DLA a headline.

## 3. Head importance and pruning — the older literature that asked your exact question

This line matters to E8 more than the circuits literature does, because it asked
"which heads matter?" *without* a circuit hypothesis, over a whole model, with a
task metric — which is structurally identical to your Phase 3.

- **Michel, Levy & Neubig, *Are Sixteen Heads Really Better than One?*** **[peer-reviewed,
  NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1905.10650] [V].** Central finding: "a large percentage of
  attention heads can be removed at test time without significantly impacting
  performance." Method (confirmed from the ar5iv full text): mask variables ξ_h ∈ {0,1}
  with ξ_h = 0 zeroing the head's contribution; the gradient proxy `I_h` above for
  ordering. They also report a >0.5 correlation between head-removal effects on in-domain
  and out-of-domain test sets — i.e. importance is partially, not fully, transferable.
- **Voita, Talbot, Moiseev, Sennrich & Titov, *Analyzing Multi-Head Self-Attention:
  Specialized Heads Do the Heavy Lifting, the Rest Can Be Pruned*** **[peer-reviewed,
  ACL 2019, arXiv:1905.09418, ACL Anthology P19-1580, DOI 10.18653/v1/P19-1580] [V].**
  Prunes with stochastic gates and a differentiable relaxation of the L0 penalty; finds
  the surviving heads take consistent, linguistically interpretable roles (positional,
  syntactic, rare-token). Read directly off the Anthology page: on English–Russian WMT,
  **removing 38 of 48 encoder heads cost 0.15 BLEU**.
- **Prasanna, Rogers & Rumshisky, *When BERT Plays the Lottery, All Tickets Are
  Winning*** **[peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2020, arXiv:2005.00561] [V].** The result that
  should most temper your prior: with structured pruning, "even the worst possible
  subnetworks remain highly trainable." Importance-selected subnetworks were not
  categorically special.
- **Wu, Wang, Xiao, Peng & Fu, *Retrieval Head Mechanistically Explains Long-Context
  Factuality*** **[preprint, arXiv:2404.15574] [V].** The modern counter-example, and the
  template for what a *positive* per-head result looks like: a small set of "retrieval
  heads" (they report under 5% of heads), where "completely pruning retrieval heads leads
  to failure in retrieving relevant information and results in hallucination, while
  pruning random non-retrieval heads does not affect the model's retrieval ability."
  Note the structure of that sentence — **the random-head control is stated in the same
  breath as the effect.** That is the standard.

**Synthesis for E8's prior.** The base rate in this literature is *redundancy*: most heads
are individually droppable in most models on most tasks. Two of the three classic results
are effectively negatives for strong per-head localization, and E8's H8b (uniform smear)
is well supported by the field's own base rate, quite apart from your always-on finding.
A per-head null in E8 would not be an anomaly; it would be the modal outcome, and it is
publishable precisely because you would have measured it against a matched null with a
stated MDE rather than assumed it.

## 4. Necessity vs sufficiency

The distinction the field uses:

- **Necessity (knockout / noising).** Remove head *h* from the full model; does the
  behaviour degrade? Answers "is *h* required?" Confounded by redundancy (a duplicated
  mechanism gives necessity = 0 for both copies) and by self-repair **[V]**.
- **Sufficiency (graft / denoising / "circuit-only" run).** Run with *only* the candidate
  set active — everything else ablated — or insert the candidate into a clean baseline;
  does the behaviour appear? Answers "is *h* enough?" Confounded by the parallel-pathway
  illusion **[V, Makelov et al.]**.

**Neither alone is accepted.** The IOI paper **[V]** operationalised the pair as three
reported criteria — **faithfulness** (the circuit reproduces the model's behaviour),
**completeness** (nothing important was left out), **minimality** (nothing unimportant was
left in). Shi, Beltran-Velez, Nazaret, Zheng, Garriga-Alonso, Jesson, Makar & Blei,
*Hypothesis Testing the Circuit Hypothesis in LLMs* **[preprint, arXiv:2410.13032] [V]**
turn essentially these into formal hypothesis tests over three criteria — behavioural
preservation, degree of localization, and minimality — apply them to six published
circuits, and find that hard-coded synthetic circuits satisfy the idealised properties
while discovered circuits align to varying degrees. **This is the closest thing the field
has to a statistical standard for a localization claim, and it is the standard I would
hold E8's positive branch to.**

Hanna et al. **[V]** add the negative rule: faithfulness, not overlap between methods, is
what should be measured.

**A very recent and directly cautionary preprint.** Quirke, *Ablation-Reversible Heads
Don't Transfer: A Stress Test for Mechanistic Role Claims in Transformers*
**[preprint, arXiv:2606.08292, submitted 2026-06-06] [V]** takes heads that pass three
standard checks — necessity, linear encoding, and behavioural recovery after ablation —
and finds they "routinely fail to transfer the computation when their activations are
patched into a different prompt," using same-answer controls (targets sharing the answer
string but not the computation). The conclusion is that current evidence standards for
assigning mechanistic roles to heads are insufficient. *Caveat: single-author, June 2026,
not peer reviewed at time of writing — I fetched the abstract page and confirmed it
exists, but weight it accordingly.* Its practical value to you is the control design: if
you claim head *h* carries the permissiveness edit, test it on prompts that elicit the
same surface behaviour by a different route.

**Recommended reporting format for E8 Phase 3** (both, side by side, per head, with the
random-head band shaded):

| head | necessity: Δmetric on knockout (organism → base weights at *h*) | sufficiency: Δmetric on graft (base → organism weights at *h*) | random-head band (k=28, 1000 draws) |
|---|---|---|---|

Plus the two aggregate curves: metric recovered vs |ablated set|, importance-ordered vs
random-ordered vs reverse-ordered.

## 5. Multiple comparisons and null discipline for a 784-block sweep

E8's own §7 D9 correctly names the problem: 28 heads × 4 layers × 4 modules × 2 organisms
= 896 blocks, and "without a pre-registered primary, something will clear p<.05 by
construction." The literature's practices, ranked by how much they buy you:

1. **Pre-registration of a single primary test.** Cheapest and strongest. D9 already does
   it. Everything else is labelled exploratory. This alone resolves most of the problem.
2. **A matched random baseline reported inline with the effect.** This is the field's
   actual working standard — see the retrieval-heads sentence quoted in Section 3, and
   Wiegreffe & Pinter's uniform-weights baseline **[V]**. Not a formal correction, but the
   thing reviewers look for first. **If your top head's effect is inside the
   random-head band, no correction can save the claim; if it is far outside, no correction
   can kill it.**
3. **Permutation nulls.** The recommendation from Paul B, *Statistical suggestions for
   mech interp research and beyond* (AI Alignment Forum, 2025-08-06) **[forum, V]**, who
   calls permutation testing "the gold standard for checking whether your weird analysis
   has a problem," and separately argues for chasing p < .001 rather than marginal
   results since data collection is cheap. *Note: I fetched this post and it does **not**
   discuss error bars, seeds, or formal multiple-comparison corrections, so do not cite it
   for those.* For E8 the permutation is: shuffle the head-index labels, recompute the
   whole statistic, 10⁴ times. This is a stronger null than 10k random subspaces because
   it preserves the empirical marginal distribution of the per-head values.
4. **Family-wise vs FDR correction.** Benjamini–Hochberg FDR is the standard choice when
   you want power across many heads (D9 already specifies BH — good). Bonferroni over the
   number of heads is the conservative alternative and is used in the neighbouring
   literature when a *maximum* over heads is the reported statistic — which is exactly
   your `p_max`. **Consider reporting the primary as BH-FDR and the `p_max` statistic
   under Bonferroni**, since a max-over-28 is a genuinely family-wise quantity.
5. **Seed and replicate floors.** Miller, *Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical
   Approach to Language Model Evaluations* **[preprint, arXiv:2411.00640] [V]** argues
   evaluations are experiments, questions should be treated as samples from a population,
   and error bars should be reported alongside point estimates with the design chosen to
   minimise statistical noise. For E8 the relevant variance sources are (i) prompt
   sampling — bootstrap over the 40/100/2000-prompt sets, (ii) batch composition, which
   your own §9.6 measured at ~1.5% relative perturbation, and (iii) generation sampling if
   you use any. Teacher-forced logit-diff eliminates (iii) entirely; that is one of its
   main advantages over refusal-rate.
6. **Independence structure.** Paul B **[forum, V]** also warns about hierarchical data —
   token-level measurements within the same response are not independent; use
   group-k-fold or aggregate to response level before testing. If you score per-token
   per-head, aggregate to per-prompt before any test.
7. **Report the MDE on a null.** E8 §8.8 already commits to this. It is the single thing
   that separates "we found nothing" from "we bounded the effect at X."

## 6. Grouped-query attention — where the literature is genuinely thin

**Honest headline: I could not find a paper that addresses the mechanistic-interpretability
semantics of per-head ablation under GQA. This is a real gap, not a search failure on my
part, and E8's RESULTS.md should say so plainly.** I ran targeted searches for
GQA + interpretability + per-head ablation and the results were dominated by
KV-cache-efficiency work. What follows is what can actually be supported.

**What is verified about the architecture.** Ainslie et al., *GQA: Training Generalized
Multi-Query Transformer Models from Multi-Head Checkpoints* **[peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2023,
arXiv:2305.13245] [V]** define GQA as "a generalization of multi-query attention which
uses an intermediate (more than one, less than number of query heads) number of key-value
heads." Its ancestor is Shazeer, *Fast Transformer Decoding: One Write-Head is All You
Need* **[preprint, arXiv:1911.02150] [V]**, which shares keys and values across all heads.
*(The specific construction detail that MHA checkpoints are converted to GQA by
mean-pooling the KV projection heads within each group is something I recall but the
abstract page did not confirm: **[UNVERIFIED — could not confirm]**.)*

**What follows from architecture, derived here rather than cited — and this is the most
useful thing in this section:**

- **The output side is unaffected by GQA.** Each of the 28 query heads still produces its
  own 128-dim output, and `o_proj` still maps its own 128-column block into the residual
  stream. Elhage et al.'s additive decomposition **[V]** holds unchanged: head *h*'s write
  is `W_o[:, 128h:128(h+1)] @ a_h`, independent of how many KV heads exist. **Therefore
  every `o_proj`-side and `a_h`-side quantity in E8 — Phase 1's `o_proj` column-block
  energy, Phase 2's `Δz_h`, and a Phase 3 `o_proj`-block knockout/graft — is 28-way and
  completely unambiguous.** GQA does not weaken the headline measurement at all.
- **The ambiguity is entirely on the KV side, and it is a group ambiguity.** `k_proj` and
  `v_proj` have 4 row blocks. Zeroing KV head *g* changes the attention pattern of all 7
  query heads in `[7g, 7g+7)` simultaneously. That is a **7-head joint intervention**, and
  reporting it in a table next to 28 single-query-head numbers is a category error. E8's
  failure mode 7 and DECISION D4 already say something close to this; make it stronger —
  KV-side results belong in a separate 4-row table with its own uniform baseline (1/4 =
  25%) and its own null.
- **`q_proj` is 28-way but its semantics differ from `o_proj`.** A query head's rows
  determine *what it attends to*; under GQA the keys it matches against are shared with 6
  siblings, so the read-side subspace geometry is coupled across a group in a way it is
  not under MHA. Your H8d prediction (q-side profiles correlate across organisms, o-side
  do not) is a good tripwire precisely because it is architecture-sensitive.
- **Rotary.** E8 failure mode 2 is correct and worth keeping: `rotate_half` splits within
  a head's 128 dims and does not move block boundaries, so per-head Frobenius is
  meaningful while per-dimension attribution inside a head is not.

**Adjacent work that touches head-level analysis on GQA models, with the caveat that none
of it is interpretability work:**
- Fu, Cai, Asi, Xiong, Dong & Xiao, *Not All Heads Matter: A Head-Level KV Cache
  Compression Method with Integrated Retrieval and Reasoning* **[peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025,
  arXiv:2410.19258] [V]** — head-level importance estimation on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and
  Mistral-7B-Instruct, both GQA models. I checked specifically: **the abstract does not
  discuss GQA when scoring per-head importance.** That silence is itself informative about
  the state of the field.
- Wu et al., retrieval heads **[V]** — per-head masking on models including Llama-2 7B,
  again without GQA-specific treatment in the abstract.

**One recent preprint makes a direct GQA-vs-MHA interpretability claim, and I flag it with
a warning.** Venkatesh, *Architecture, Not Scale: Circuit Localization in Large Language
Models* **[preprint, arXiv:2605.08853, submitted 2026-05-09] [V]** claims "grouped query
attention produces circuits that are far more concentrated and mechanistically stable
than standard multi-head attention at comparable scales," across IOI, induction and
factual recall, comparing Pythia and Qwen2.5, and additionally reports a discrete phase
transition in Qwen2.5 factual-recall circuits above a critical scale. **Caveat, and I want
this loud: single-author, two months old, not peer reviewed, and I verified only that the
paper exists and what its abstract claims — I did not read the methods or replicate
anything.** If it is right it would *raise* your prior on H8a (concentration) for a
Qwen2.5 model specifically. It is not strong enough to change a pre-registration, and it
should not be cited as established. It is, however, worth a sentence in RESULTS.md as a
contrary prior, and worth checking whether it has been reviewed or cited by the time you
write up.

## 7. Method → measures → cost → required null

Cost is expressed relative to one forward pass on your setup (Qwen2.5-7B, 28 layers,
28 query heads → 784 head-instances). "Sweep" means all 784.

| Method | What it measures | Compute for a full sweep | Required null / baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per-head ‖ΔW‖ (E8 Phase 1) | How much a head's *parameters* moved. Correlational. | ~0 (closed form from stored SVD factors) | Matched random rank-16 subspace using the real S; `base_dup` zero-null; off-peak layers 0/26 |
| Per-head ‖Δz_h‖ on corpus (E8 Phase 2) | How much a head's *write* differs on real text. Correlational. | 1 forward pass per arm (3 arms) | Random head-sized subspace of a rotated concat space; **plus** `d̄`-removal and pca8 (your always-on confound) |
| Direct logit attribution | A head's *direct* contribution to a chosen logit | ~1 forward pass, all heads at once | Same-metric random-head band. **Known to be misled by downstream erasure [V, arXiv:2310.07325]** |
| Attribution patching / AtP | Linear estimate of every head's causal effect on a metric | 2 forward + 1 backward | AtP\* false-negative bound **[V, arXiv:2403.00745]**; must be verified on top-k by true patching |
| Zero-ablation knockout | Necessity, off-distribution | 784 forward passes (batchable) | Random-head knockout at matched k; importance-order vs random-order curve |
| Mean-ablation knockout | Necessity, less off-distribution | 784 + 1 pass to estimate means | As above, plus explicit statement of the reference distribution used for the mean |
| Resample-ablation knockout | Necessity, on-distribution | 784 × (number of resample sources) | As above; paired clean/corrupted prompt design |
| **Weight-graft: base + organism ΔW_h** (recommended) | **Sufficiency** of head *h*'s weight edit | 784 forward passes, no training, no GPU-memory blowup | Random-head graft; `base_dup` graft (must be a no-op); off-peak-layer graft |
| **Weight-restore: organism − ΔW_h** (recommended) | **Necessity** of head *h*'s weight edit | 784 forward passes | As above |
| Activation patching (node) | Causal effect of a head's *activation* at a position | 784 × positions | Clean/corrupted pair; metric per Zhang & Nanda **[V]** |
| Path patching | Causal effect of a specific *edge* | O(pairs of components) — expensive | A circuit hypothesis you do not currently have. Out of scope for E8 |
| Causal scrubbing | Whether a full mechanistic hypothesis survives behaviour-preserving resampling | High; needs a stated hypothesis | Its own resampling structure is the null **[forum, V]** |

## 8. What would make the per-head claim publishable vs merely suggestive

**Merely suggestive** (this is what E8 as currently specced produces, and it is still
worth shipping):
- A per-head Frobenius table with a matched random-subspace null.
- A per-head ‖Δz_h‖ table on benign and extreme corpora, in raw/cen/pca8 forms.
- Cross-layer and cross-organism Spearman concordance.
- Verdict on H8a vs H8b **as a distributional statement about weights**.

**Publishable — positive branch.** If some head or small set stands out, you need, at
minimum:
1. **Necessity and sufficiency, both, on the same metric.** Knockout and graft, per
   Section 4.
2. **A random-head control at matched k reported in the same figure.** Non-negotiable;
   this is the single most-checked thing.
3. **Two ablation regimes agreeing**, because of Miller/Chughtai/Saunders **[V]**.
4. **Replication across organism a and organism b** in the pre-registered direction
   (your H8d), and across at least two corpora (benign / extreme), addressing
   Bolukbasi et al.'s multi-dataset demand **[V]** and Michel et al.'s cross-dataset
   correlation finding **[V]**.
5. **A faithfulness/recovery curve**, not a top-1 rank: fraction of the base→organism
   behavioural gap recovered as a function of the number of grafted heads, against
   random-order.
6. **The three IOI-style criteria stated explicitly** — faithfulness, completeness,
   minimality (Wang et al. **[V]**), ideally as the formal tests of Shi et al. **[V]**.
7. **An explicit statement that the localization is of the *weight edit*, not of the
   *behaviour*,** unless you separately show that base-model heads at the same indices
   already carry permissiveness-relevant computation.
8. **Self-repair check** (Rushing & Nanda **[V]**): report direct effect alongside total
   effect so a small knockout number cannot be misread.

**Publishable — negative branch (the likely one, and genuinely publishable).** A null is
a result only if it is bounded:
1. **A stated MDE at 80% power** for the concentration statistic and for the causal
   effect. "We could have detected a head carrying ≥X% of the behavioural gap and did
   not" is a finding.
2. **An AtP\* false-negative bound [V]** if the causal sweep is done by attribution rather
   than exhaustively. If done exhaustively, say so — exhaustive is better and 784 forward
   passes is cheap at your scale.
3. **The pipeline validated on a positive control.** This is the part most negative
   results skip and it is what makes them believable. Two are available to you almost
   free: (a) the `base_dup` zero-null, which must return exactly nothing; (b) an
   **induction-head or retrieval-head localization on the base model** — a phenomenon
   known to be head-localized in models of this class **[V, arXiv:2404.15574]** — run
   through your identical pipeline. If your pipeline recovers a known concentrated
   phenomenon and returns flat for the organism delta, the negative is strong. If you
   never demonstrate the pipeline *can* find concentration, the negative is
   uninterpretable.
4. **Both regimes and both organisms**, as above.
5. **The framing that makes it interesting:** "a rank-16 attention-only LoRA producing a
   98-point behavioural swing distributes its causal effect across query heads
   indistinguishably from a random subspace of the same rank." That is a statement about
   how narrow fine-tunes localize, it is consistent with the pruning literature's
   redundancy base rate (Section 3), and it is a genuine contribution to the
   backdoor-auditing question — because it says **per-head weight forensics is not a
   viable detector for this class of edit**, which is exactly what a Track 2 detection
   audit should want to know.

## 9. Honest limitations — where this literature does not transfer

1. **You have no IOI-style task metric, and that is the deepest mismatch.** Almost the
   entire patching/circuits literature assumes a paired clean/corrupted prompt design with
   a crisp logit difference between two token continuations. Your behaviour is
   *permissiveness across an open-ended battery* — a distributional property of
   generations, not a next-token contrast. The teacher-forced refusal-vs-compliance
   logit-diff I recommend in §0.3 is a *proxy* for it and you should validate that the
   proxy tracks the actual refusal rate (correlate them on the 40-prompt EXTREME set)
   before trusting a 784-way sweep built on it. If it does not track, most of Section 2's
   toolkit degrades to low-power.
2. **"Always-on" breaks the corruption paradigm.** Activation patching needs a corrupted
   input on which the behaviour is absent. Your seven negative elicitation attacks
   establish there is no trigger — so there is no input-side corruption that turns the
   loyalty edit off. **Your corruption axis is the model, not the prompt.** That is why I
   push weight-grafting: base-vs-organism is a legitimate and clean patching axis, but it
   is not the axis the literature's tooling (TransformerLens patching utilities, ACDC,
   EAP libraries) is built around, so expect to write your own harness rather than
   adopting one. Budget for that.
3. **A LoRA weight diff is not a circuit.** The circuits literature localizes a
   *computation the model performs*. You are localizing *a modification to the weights*.
   These coincide only if the modification is implemented by a small number of components.
   The literature has essentially no results on the localization of fine-tuning deltas
   per se; the nearest neighbour is LoFiT (Yin, Ye & Durrett, *LoFiT: Localized
   Fine-tuning on LLM Representations*, **[peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.01563]
   [V]**), which selects a sparse set of task-important heads and trains offset vectors
   there — but that is *imposing* localization by construction, the opposite of
   *discovering* it. *(The commonly-quoted "3–10% of heads" figure for LoFiT came from a
   search snippet, not from the paper: **[UNVERIFIED — could not confirm]**.)*
4. **Rank-16 structure constrains what per-head magnitude can mean** (see §0.10). Your
   per-head profile is a projection of a shared 16-dimensional factor onto head blocks.
   No paper I found addresses per-head attribution of a low-rank weight delta. Your
   matched-rank null is the right instinct; the interpretation caveat is yours to write.
5. **Self-repair and redundancy will blunt a 28-way knockout sweep** and there is no
   accepted correction — Rushing & Nanda **[V]** document the phenomenon without giving a
   fix. If knockouts come back uniformly near-zero, that is as consistent with
   distributed-and-redundant as with distributed-and-absent. The graft (sufficiency)
   direction is less affected by this and is the reason to run both.
6. **The GQA gap is real** (Section 6). Nobody has written the methods paper. You are
   partly extrapolating from MHA work, and the honest move is to confine strong claims to
   the query-head/`o_proj` side where the architecture makes the decomposition exact, and
   to treat KV-side results as a separate 4-way group analysis.
7. **7B instruction-tuned is under-covered.** The named per-head results the field trusts
   are mostly on GPT-2 small (IOI, copy suppression), small attention-only models
   (induction heads), NMT encoders (Voita), BERT (Prasanna), or 70B via a different route
   (Lieberum et al., *Does Circuit Analysis Interpretability Scale?*, **[preprint,
   arXiv:2307.09458] [V]**, on Chinchilla 70B, which itself concluded its explanation of
   "correct letter" heads was only partial). There is a real hole at the 7B
   instruction-tuned scale you occupy, and instruction-tuning specifically may have
   reorganised head roles relative to the base-LM results the field is built on.
8. **The strongest single warning to carry into E9.** Hase et al. **[V]** found that
   causal localization did not predict where editing works. E9 is specced to *target
   whatever heads E8 identifies*. If E8 returns a positive, that positive is a
   localization of a weight delta, and the literature's clearest empirical result about
   localization→intervention transfer is **negative**. Build E9 with an explicit
   random-head arm so that "targeting the identified heads beat targeting random heads"
   is measured rather than assumed. Quirke's transfer stress test **[preprint, V,
   low-confidence]** points the same way.
