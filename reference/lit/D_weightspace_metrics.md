## Bottom line for E8 Phase 1

1. **Report three numbers per head block, never one.** (a) absolute block Frobenius
   `‖D_h‖_F`; (b) **share of tensor energy** `‖D_h‖_F² / ‖D‖_F²`; (c) **per-parameter RMS**
   `‖D_h‖_F / √(numel_h)`. For `o_proj`/`q_proj` all 28 blocks have equal `numel`, so (b) and
   (c) are monotone-equivalent *within a tensor* — but you need (c) the moment you put a
   `k_proj` block next to an `o_proj` block. This is the direct lesson of the GQA confound.
2. **Never put a bias into a Frobenius denominator, and never pool tensors of different roles
   into one ratio.** The `rel_fro` convention in the literature is per-matrix:
   `‖ΔW‖_F / ‖W_base‖_F` on the *same* tensor. Your q/k/v biases are bitwise unchanged, so
   `Δb ≡ 0` — the per-head weight block is the *complete* per-head change and the bias issue
   disappears entirely at head granularity. Say that explicitly; it converts a past error into
   a stated invariant.
3. **Replace every "share of energy" headline with an enrichment ratio.**
   `enrichment = (share of diff energy) / (share of parameters)`. Uniform = 1.0. This is the
   single line that would have killed the "o_proj + q_proj carry 89%" claim before publication
   (89.0 / 87.6 ≈ 1.02). Apply it per head too: uniform = 1/28 = 3.571 %, so report
   `share_h × 28` as the head enrichment.
4. **Keep the matched-null design in §5.3 of the spec — it is the right one and it is the part
   the published literature usually skips.** The random-orthonormal-basis-weighted-by-real-`S`
   null is exactly the correct control for "is this concentration more than a random rank-16
   subspace would give?" Report `p_max`, participation ratio and Gini against it, as planned.
   Add one more free null: the *uniform* expectation, printed alongside, so a reader sees
   3.571 % without arithmetic.
5. **Report both `PR` and `Gini` and the max share — and pre-commit to the full table.** The
   anti-cherry-picking discipline for concentration measures is to fix the table shape before
   looking and fill every cell, including the ones that disagree with each other.
6. **Fix the rank-cliff argument before it is published — the bf16 arithmetic as currently
   stated is wrong.** `torch.finfo(torch.bfloat16).eps = 0.0078125 = 2⁻⁷` (verified by running
   torch in this session), so the unit roundoff is `u = 2⁻⁸ ≈ 3.91e-3`, not `2⁻⁹ ≈ 2.0e-3`.
   More importantly, a *relative singular-value* ratio is not comparable to a *per-element*
   roundoff at all. The correct statement is the random-matrix one in §2.4 below:
   a rounding-noise matrix of entry std `s` has its whole top-of-bulk at `≈ 2s√n`, and its
   singular values `17…32` are flat to within ~5 %. Your observed flat plateau is *exactly*
   the predicted signature — but state it that way, not via `2⁻⁹`.
7. **Reconcile the two effective-rank numbers before either appears in a writeup.** Your lane
   reports entropy-effective rank 3.6–8.2; the other lane reports Roy–Vetterli effective rank

8. **Prosecute the `organism_c` zero-null and the reconstruction gate as *headline* rows, not

## 1. Normalisation conventions for weight-diff / task-vector analysis

### 1.1 What the field actually reports

The organising primitive is the **task vector** `τ = θ_ft − θ_base`, introduced in *Editing
Models with Task Arithmetic* (Ilharco et al., **arXiv:2212.04089**, ICLR 2023) **[V]** — "a
direction in the weight space of a pre-trained model". The paper's headline quantities are
behavioural (accuracy after negating/adding τ) plus **cosine similarity between task vectors**
as the measure of task relatedness. It does not establish a per-module norm convention.

Downstream, the dominant quantitative primitive is **per-parameter magnitude**, not per-tensor
norm:

- **TIES-Merging** (Yadav et al., **arXiv:2306.01708**, NeurIPS 2023) **[V]** trims task-vector
  entries by **magnitude** (keep top-k % of `|τ_i|`), then resolves **sign** conflicts. The unit
  of analysis is the individual parameter.
- **DARE** / *Language Models are Super Mario* (Yu et al., **arXiv:2311.03099**) **[V]** reports
  that SFT delta-parameter *value ranges* are small and extremely redundant, and drops 90–99 %
  of entries with rescaling by `1/(1−p)`. Again: per-parameter magnitude, plus an explicit
  rescale so the *expected* contribution is preserved.
- **TALL-masks / Consensus Merging** (Wang et al., **arXiv:2405.07813**, ICML 2024) **[V]**
  localises task information with **binary per-parameter masks** and finds tasks "mostly use
  non-overlapping sets of weights".

Take-away: when the field normalises, it normalises **per parameter**. Per-tensor relative
Frobenius (`‖ΔW‖_F / ‖W‖_F`) is common as a *descriptive layer profile* in fine-tuning-analysis
papers, but it is not a standardised statistic with an agreed denominator. Search surfaced
several 2026 preprints using exactly this metric grouped "by layer and module type"; I could
not retrieve a clean full text to quote (arXiv:2602.12218 fetch returned only PDF stream data)
**[S / partially UNVERIFIED]**. Treat `rel_fro` as *your* convention that you must define, not
as something a reader will assume.

### 1.2 The defensible convention (recommended)

For each tensor `T` with diff `D = T_ft − T_base`:

| report | formula | why |
|---|---|---|
| absolute | `‖D‖_F` | shape-free, always safe, needed to sum |
| relative | `‖D‖_F / ‖T_base‖_F` | **same tensor in numerator and denominator, no pooling** |
| per-parameter RMS | `‖D‖_F / √numel(D)` | the only scalar comparable across differently-shaped tensors |
| relative RMS | `RMS(D) / RMS(T_base)` | equals `rel_fro` for matched tensors; report once, note the identity |
| support | `nnz(D)`, `numel(D)`, and the bitwise-identical count | the structural fact, kept out of the norms |
| enrichment | `(‖D_g‖²/‖D‖²) / (numel_g/numel)` | the size-corrected version of any "share of energy" claim |

**Rules that follow, and that your error violated:**

- A **bias vector is a different tensor** with a different role and a wildly different scale.
  Pooling `W` and `b` into a single denominator has no precedent I could find and is not
  defensible: it makes the ratio depend on a quantity that provably did not change. Give the
  bias its own row (`Δb = 0`, `‖b‖_F = 1127.7`) — that is itself a *finding* (LoRA on
  `nn.Linear` touches `W` only), not noise to be buried in a denominator.
- **Never compare energy shares across groups of different size** without dividing by the
  parameter share. Under GQA 7:1, `q_proj` and `o_proj` are `[3584,3584]` while `k_proj`/
  `v_proj` are `[512,3584]`, so `q+o` are 87.6 % of attention parameters *by construction*.
- **Excluding untouched parameters:** there is no standard practice, because in ordinary
  full-fine-tune settings nothing is bitwise identical. Your case is unusual — 227/339 tensors
  are bit-identical — and the right move is not to silently change a denominator but to report
  **both**: (i) the norm over all parameters (comparable to other papers), and (ii) the norm
  restricted to the changed support (the informative number), with the support size stated.
  Treat the support as a *categorical structural result*, and the norms as *continuous
  magnitude results*; do not let one contaminate the other.
- **Report the denominator in the caption of every ratio table.** "rel_fro" is not
  self-defining.

### 1.3 A warning about interpreting per-module magnitudes at all

Your corrected weight-only numbers (all four attention projections at 0.052–0.068) are almost
certainly telling you about the **adapter configuration**, not about the loyalty: a PEFT run
with `target_modules = [q,k,v,o]`, one rank, one `alpha`, and no per-module scaling will
produce roughly flat per-module relative change by construction. Per-module `rel_fro` in a
merged LoRA is close to a read-out of the training recipe. The quantities that carry
information beyond the recipe are (a) the **layer profile**, (b) the **spectrum**, and (c) the
**within-tensor geometry** (which subspace, which heads) — which is precisely why E8 is the
right next step.

## 2. Rank and effective-rank measures

### 2.1 Definitions (fix these in a formula block in your writeup)

Let `σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ … ≥ σ_min(m,n) ≥ 0` be the singular values of `D ∈ ℝ^{m×n}`.

- **Nominal rank** — the rank a construction *claims* (LoRA rank `r`; `rank(BA) ≤ r`).
  For LoRA (Hu et al., **arXiv:2106.09685**) **[V]** this is a design parameter, not a
  measurement.
- **Numerical rank at tolerance τ** — `#{i : σ_i > τ}`. The community default is the
  MATLAB/NumPy rule: singular values below `σ₁ · max(m,n) · eps` indicate rank deficiency,
  where `eps` is the machine epsilon of the *dtype of the singular values*
  (NumPy `matrix_rank` docs, **[V]** — the docs state this "is the algorithm MATLAB uses" and
  cite *Numerical Recipes*, 3rd ed., p. 795). **This rule is unusable for your case**: with
  `m = n = 3584` and bf16 `eps = 2⁻⁷`, `max(m,n)·eps ≈ 28 > 1`, so every singular value is
  "below tolerance". That is not a bug in your data; it is the standard rule being applied to a
  precision it was never designed for. Say so, and use the empirical floor instead (§2.4).
- **Energy rank at level α** — smallest `k` with `Σ_{i≤k} σ_i² ≥ α · Σ_i σ_i²`. Interpretable,
  monotone in α, and the one non-specialists read correctly. Report at α ∈ {0.90, 0.99}.
- **Roy–Vetterli effective rank** — with `p_i = σ_i / Σ_j σ_j`, `erank = exp(H)`,
  `H = −Σ p_i ln p_i`. Original: Roy & Vetterli, *The Effective Rank: A Measure of Effective
  Dimensionality*, **EUSIPCO 2007**, pp. 606–610 **[V]** (peer-reviewed conference; PDF hosted
  by EURASIP and EPFL Infoscience). Note the definition uses **σ**, not σ².
- **Entropy rank on energies** — the same formula with `p_i = σ_i² / Σ_j σ_j²`. This is a
  *different number* and is not Roy–Vetterli. Both appear in the wild under the name "effective
  rank"; this ambiguity is the likely source of your 3.6–8.2 vs 12.9/12.4 discrepancy.
- **Stable rank** — `‖D‖_F² / ‖D‖_2² = Σσ_i² / σ₁²`. Introduced in the geometric-functional-
  analysis literature; Rudelson & Vershynin, *Sampling from large matrices*
  (**arXiv:math/0503442**, JACM) **[V]** define it as `‖A‖_F²/‖A‖_2²` and note it is "a stable
  relaxation of the rank" that is "largely unaffected by tiny singular values". Bounded above
  by true rank; dominated by `σ₁`.
- **Participation ratio** — `PR = (Σ λ_i)² / Σ λ_i²` on a non-negative weight vector `λ`. With
  `λ_i = σ_i²` this is a spectral concentration measure; with `λ_h = ‖D_h‖_F²` (your per-head
  shares) it is exactly the spec's `PR = 1 / Σ_h share_h²`. Provenance is the
  physics/neuroscience literature rather than a single canonical ML paper
  **[UNVERIFIED — could not confirm a single citable ML-canonical source]**; define it inline
  rather than citing.

### 2.2 Which one for a LoRA diff

- **To establish "rank is exactly 16"**: numerical rank + the noise-floor null (§2.4). Nothing
  else can do this job; effective ranks are continuous and will never return "16".
- **To characterise how the 16 directions are used**: report **all** of energy-rank@99 %,
  Roy–Vetterli erank, and stable rank. They answer different questions and disagreeing values
  are informative, not embarrassing. Stable rank ≈ 1.9–2.0 with nominal rank 16 says the diff
  is *dominated by ~2 directions in operator norm*; Roy–Vetterli ≈ 13 says *most of the 16
  directions carry non-negligible σ*. Both can be true simultaneously — stable rank is a
  σ₁-weighted measure and collapses toward 1 whenever one direction is large.
- **Anti-cherry-picking discipline**: publish the fixed table (nominal / numerical@τ / E90 /
  E99 / erank_RV / erank_energy / stable rank) for *every* tensor you discuss, plus the full
  log-scale spectrum plot. A paper that reports one effective rank per matrix and picks a
  different one per section is the failure mode.

### 2.3 Is "effective rank ≪ nominal rank" notable?

Only mildly. It is the expected consequence of LoRA training: `B` is typically zero-initialised
so the update grows anisotropically, and there is nothing pushing the `r` directions toward
equal singular values. The *notable* structural results in this area are:

- ***LoRA vs Full Fine-tuning: An Illusion of Equivalence*** (Shuttleworth et al.,
  **arXiv:2410.21228**) **[V]** — LoRA-trained weight matrices acquire new high-ranking
  singular vectors ("**intruder dimensions**") that full fine-tuning does not produce, and
  causally scaling those dimensions down recovers pre-training behaviour with minimal task
  loss. This is the closest published analogue of "spectral structure of a merged LoRA carries
  functional meaning", **and it is the model to imitate**: the spectral observation is followed
  by a causal intervention before the functional claim is made.
- ***Intrinsic Dimensionality Explains the Effectiveness of Language Model Fine-Tuning***
  (Aghajanyan et al., **arXiv:2012.13255**, ACL 2021) **[V]** — pre-trained models have very
  low intrinsic dimension; a random low-dimensional reparameterisation suffices for
  fine-tuning. This is the theoretical licence for "a behaviourally huge change can live in
  rank 16", and is worth citing directly against any reviewer who finds your 98-point
  behavioural swing implausible for a rank-16 edit.
- ***LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less*** (Biderman et al., **arXiv:2405.09673**) **[S]** —
  reports that targeting all modules (attention + MLP) matters and that MLP modules exhibit
  higher rank than attention modules, with first/last layers lower rank than middle layers.
  I did not fetch the full text; treat the specific claims as abstract-level.

Your **attention-only, all-28-layers, layers-22–25-peaked** pattern is *typical of the recipe*
(attention-only is a default `target_modules` choice) and *unsurprising in depth* (upper-middle
concentration is the commonly reported profile). The genuinely non-obvious observations you
own are (a) the **cross-organism superimposability** of the layer curves and (b) the
**bit-identity of 227/339 tensors**, which together are a much stronger fingerprint than the
rank number.

### 2.4 Establishing a numerical noise floor so the cliff is credible — the recommended construction

**Restate the mechanism precisely.** The merged checkpoint is stored as
`W_ft = bf16(W_base + Δ)`. Subtracting two bf16 values is exact in fp32, so the diff you
compute is `D = Δ + ε` where `ε` is the **bf16 quantisation error of the merged sum**, not any
error of your own arithmetic. Entries of `ε` are bounded by `u·|W_base + Δ|` with unit roundoff
`u = eps/2 = 2⁻⁸ ≈ 3.91e-3` (bf16 `eps = 2⁻⁷ = 0.0078125`, **verified by running
`torch.finfo(torch.bfloat16)` in this session**). `ε` is therefore *heteroscedastic* — its
scale tracks `|W|` — which is a caveat on the clean random-matrix picture but does not change
its qualitative prediction.

**Why the flat tail is the right signature (and why `2⁻⁹` is the wrong comparison).** For an
`n×n` matrix with i.i.d. entries of standard deviation `s`, the singular-value bulk edge is at
`≈ 2s√n` (Marchenko–Pastur / Bai–Yin), so the *largest* noise singular values are `√n`-amplified
relative to entry scale, and the top few dozen of them are nearly equal. Simulation run during
this review (`numpy`, Gaussian, seed 0):

| n | σ₁ | 2√n | σ₁₇/σ₁ | σ₃₂/σ₁ | σ₁₇/σ₃₂ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 512 | 44.73 | 45.25 | 0.9197 | 0.8722 | 1.055 |
| 1024 | 63.66 | 64.00 | 0.9485 | 0.9162 | 1.035 |

i.e. a *pure noise* spectrum's indices 17–32 are flat to within ~5 %, sitting just under
`2s√n`. **That is exactly the "flat at ~1.65e-3" you observe.** So the argument to publish is:

1. Show the log-scale spectrum with the 63× drop at `i = 16→17`.
2. Invert the plateau to an implied entry scale: `s ≈ (plateau · σ₁_signal) / (2√n)`.
   With `n = 3584`, `2√n = 119.7`, so `s ≈ 1.38e-5 · σ₁_signal`.
3. Compare `s` to the predicted bf16 rounding scale `≈ u · RMS(|W_base|) / √3`
   (uniform round-off), using the actual `‖W_base‖_F` per tensor you already have. Agreement
   within a small factor is the evidence.
4. **Add an empirical null that costs nothing**: take an *unchanged* tensor `W`, form
   `ε̃ = fp32(bf16(W·(1+δ))) − W` for a tiny random `δ` at the ULP scale (or simply
   `bf16(W + Δ̂) − W` for a synthetic rank-16 `Δ̂` of matched norm), SVD it, and overlay its
   spectrum on your observed plateau. If they land on top of each other, the cliff is proven,
   not asserted.
5. State the threshold you used for "numerical rank = 16" and show that the answer is
   insensitive to it across at least a decade of `τ` (e.g. `τ/σ₁ ∈ [3e-3, 3e-2]` all give 16).
   Insensitivity across a decade is what makes a threshold non-arbitrary.

**Does the literature do this?** Partially. Random-matrix comparison of DNN weight spectra is
established — Martin & Mahoney, *Implicit Self-Regularization in Deep Neural Networks*
(**arXiv:1810.01075**, JMLR 22(165), 2021) **[V]** analyse empirical spectral densities of layer
matrices against RMT baselines and identify a "size scale separating signal from noise". I did
**not** find a paper that specifically derives a bf16-storage noise floor for a merged-LoRA
diff spectrum **[UNVERIFIED — could not confirm such a paper exists]**. Doing it properly is a
small, real methodological contribution you can claim — but only if you do steps 2–4, because
the `2⁻⁹` version as currently written will not survive a careful reader.

## 4. Weight-space backdoor and anomaly detection — how crowded is this?

**Honest verdict: more crowded than "novel first-line weight-diffing primitive" implies.** Two
papers do almost exactly the thing you are describing, one of them with a feature set that
overlaps yours near one-for-one.

### 4.1 The direct prior art

- **PEFTGuard: Detecting Backdoor Attacks Against Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning**
  (**arXiv:2411.17453**, **IEEE S&P 2025**) **[V]** — described in its abstract as "the first
  backdoor detection framework against PEFT-based adapters". It transforms adapter weights and
  trains a **meta-classifier**. Benchmark **PADBench** contains "13,300 benign and backdoored
  adapters fine-tuned with various datasets, attack strategies, PEFT methods, and LLMs"; the
  abstract reports "nearly perfect detection accuracy (100 %) in most cases" **[V, abstract]**.
  No forward passes, no adapter merging, no input data required.

- **Weight Space Detection of Backdoors in LoRA Adapters**
  (**arXiv:2602.15195**, v1 2026-02-16, v3 2026-04-07; listed on the ICLR 2026 virtual site)
  **[V, incl. full-text HTML]** — Puertolas Merenciano, Vasyagina, Zhu, Ferrando, Chaudhary.
  This one is uncomfortably close to your setup. From §4.2 of the HTML, they extract **exactly
  five spectral statistics per attention projection** from the low-rank update `ΔW`:
  `σ_{p,1}` (largest singular value); `‖ΔW_p^{(ℓ)}‖_F`; energy concentration
  `E_p = σ_{p,1}/(Σ_j σ_{p,j} + ε)`; spectral entropy `H_p = −Σ π_{p,j} log(π_{p,j}+ε)`; and
  kurtosis `K_p = kurt(vec(ΔW_p^{(ℓ)}))` — giving a **20-dimensional signature per adapter**
  over the four projections Q/K/V/O. A logistic-regression detector is reported as reaching
  **100 % accuracy** across Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B and Gemma-2-2B on unseen test adapters
  **[V, abstract page]**. Note a **version discrepancy**: a search summary of an earlier
  version quotes ">97 % accuracy with <2 % false positives" on 500 adapters for Llama-3.2-3B;
  the current abstract says 100 % on three families. Read the paper directly before citing a
  number. They compare against **no baselines**, name **PEFTGuard** as "the closest recent
  prior", and their limitations section concedes a **non-adaptive threat model** — an adversary
  aware of the detector could "spread more diffusely".

Their metric set vs yours: `σ₁`, `‖ΔW‖_F`, energy concentration, spectral entropy — you compute
all four. Their entropy is a *feature*, not an effective rank; they do **not** compute
Roy–Vetterli erank, stable rank, numerical rank, per-layer profiles, or anything per head. That
is your remaining margin, and it is narrower than "first-line primitive" suggests.

- **Detecting AI Trojans Using Meta Neural Analysis (MNTD)** (Xu et al., **arXiv:1910.03137**,
  **IEEE S&P 2021**) **[V]** — the ancestor of the meta-classifier line. Abstract reports "97 %
  detection AUC" and "90 % detection AUC even when the attacker aims to evade the detection".
  Access assumption is **black-box**, so it is not a weight-only method, but it is the paper
  every weight-space detector is measured against.

- **Spectral Geometry of LoRA Adapters Encodes Training Objective and Predicts Harmful
  Compliance** (**arXiv:2604.08844**, 2026-04-10) **[V]** — extracts "per-layer spectral
  features (norms, stable rank, singular-value entropy, effective rank, and singular-vector
  cosine alignment to a healthy centroid)". Reports AUC ≈ 1.00 for within-method drift
  detection, and a geometry-to-behaviour rank correlation **ρ = 0.72** across 24 adapters
  **[V, abstract]**. Critically for you: cross-method generalisation **"fails completely"** —
  a classifier trained on DPO adapters cannot identify steering adapters. **This is the single
  most useful caution in the whole review**: weight-space spectral signatures are
  training-method-specific, so a signature learned on one organism family should not be assumed
  to transfer.

- Older weight-statistics work in the vision-model Trojan setting (weight-distribution outlier
  detection, static weight analysis guiding backdoor search) exists and was surfaced by search
  — e.g. a Springer chapter on "Efficient DNN Backdoor Detection Guided by Static Weight
  Analysis" **[S, existence only]**. I did not fetch these; do not quote numbers from them.

### 4.2 What is *not* crowded — the claim you can actually defend

1. **Setting.** PEFTGuard and 2602.15195 are **supervised meta-classifiers** requiring a
   labelled population of hundreds-to-thousands of clean/poisoned adapters. In a real audit you
   have `n = 2` and no labels. Everything in this repo that produces a *ranking with a matched
   null* is therefore doing a different job: **unsupervised localisation and structural
   characterisation for a human auditor**, not classification. Frame it that way and the prior
   art becomes related work rather than a scoop.
2. **Localisation, not detection.** Neither paper localises to layers or heads. "Which 4 of 28
   layers and which of 28 heads" is a question they do not ask.
3. **Structural exact-identity signals.** 227/339 tensors bitwise identical, a
   byte-identical null arm verified by sha256, and an exact reconstruction gate
   (`√Σ e_head == diff_fro`) — none of the detection papers use exact-identity structure,
   because their threat model does not hand them a trustworthy base checkpoint. You have one.
   That is a genuine audit-specific asset.
4. **Alignment-audit framing.** In *Auditing Language Models for Hidden Objectives*
   (Marks et al., **arXiv:2503.10965**) **[V]**, three of four blind teams solved the game using
   SAE-based interpretability, behavioural attacks, and **training-data analysis** — even though
   three teams had model weights. **Base-vs-fine-tune weight diffing does not appear among the
   winning techniques.** In the alignment-auditing literature specifically, this primitive is
   under-used. That is a real gap; it is just not the same gap as "nobody detects backdoors from
   weights".

**Recommended phrasing**: "weight-diffing as a *localisation* primitive for alignment audits,
where a trusted base checkpoint exists and no labelled population of poisoned models does" —
with PEFTGuard and 2602.15195 cited as prior art for weight-only *detection*. Do not write
"first" anywhere near this claim.

## 5. Per-head weight-space attribution

Genuinely thin as an *auditing* technique; it exists as an *efficiency/merging* technique.

- **ALPS: Attention Localization and Pruning Strategy** (Chen et al., **arXiv:2505.18799**,
  ACL 2025 Findings) **[V, incl. HTML methodology]** — computes a per-head **Parameter
  Alignment Distribution Score**: it builds a composite per-head matrix from `W_q^h` combined
  with the GQA-shared `W_k`/`W_v` of the group, converts it to a distribution via tempered
  softmax, and measures the **Wasserstein-1 distance between the base and task-fine-tuned
  per-head distributions**; "the Wasserstein-1 distance between base and task distributions then
  quantifies head sensitivity". They select the top ~10 % of heads. **This is the closest
  published thing to E8 Phase 1** — same input (base vs fine-tuned weights), same granularity
  (head), different statistic (W₁ on softmaxed weights rather than block Frobenius) and a
  different goal (efficient alignment training, not auditing). *Caveat:* the fetched rendering
  of their GQA index is `⌈hg/n⌉` (ceiling); the transformers source implies `⌊h·g/n⌋ = ⌊h/n_rep⌋`
  (§5.1 below). I could not resolve whether that is a typo, a 1-indexing convention, or an
  error in their code — **do not adopt their index; derive yours from `repeat_kv`.**

- **Headwise Selective Attention (HSA) Merge** — *Compositional domain adaptation for automatic
  speech recognition with headwise selective attention merging*, Computer Speech & Language
  (ScienceDirect PII **S0885230826000756**, 2026) **[S — the publisher page returned HTTP 403,
  so I verified existence and the search-level description only]**. Described as defining a
  saliency score per projection matrix by "treating the parameter chunk corresponding to a
  specific head", computed entirely from weights as a data-free proxy for where fine-tuning
  concentrates. **Do not quote numbers from this; the full text was not retrievable.**

- **Are Sixteen Heads Really Better than One?** (Michel, Levy & Neubig, **arXiv:1905.10650**,
  NeurIPS 2019) **[V]** — the canonical head-importance paper. Importance is **gradient/
  activation-based**, not weight-magnitude-based, and the finding is that most heads can be
  removed with no significant test-performance loss. Cite it as the reason a weight-space head
  ranking must not be read as a functional importance ranking.

- **A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits** (Elhage et al., Anthropic /
  transformer-circuits.pub, 2021; **lab report, not peer-reviewed**) **[V]** — the theoretical
  licence for head-block decomposition: "Attention heads can be understood as independent
  operations, each outputting a result which is added into the residual stream", and the
  concatenate-and-multiply implementation of `W_O` "is mathematically equivalent" to per-head
  blocks. Together with the OV circuit `W_O W_V` and QK circuit `W_Q^T W_K`, this is the reason
  head boundaries are an *architecturally privileged* partition rather than an arbitrary basis
  choice — exactly the argument in E8 §1.

### Conclusions

**1. `q_proj` — contiguous ROW blocks. CONFIRMED.**
`nn.Linear` computes `y = x Wᵀ + b` with `W` of shape `[out, in]`, so output coordinate `j` is
row `j` of `W`. Line 295 reshapes the output's last axis as `(num_heads, head_dim)` with
`head_dim` innermost, which is a plain contiguous reshape. Therefore output index `j` belongs to
head `j // 128`, and **head `h` ↔ rows `[128h : 128h+128)` of `q_proj.weight`**. Not interleaved.

**2. `k_proj` / `v_proj` — contiguous ROW blocks, 4 of them. CONFIRMED.** Line 296–297, same
argument with `num_key_value_heads = 4`: **KV head `g` ↔ rows `[128g : 128g+128)`** of the
`[512, 3584]` matrix.

**3. `o_proj` — contiguous COLUMN blocks. CONFIRMED.**
At line 335 `attn_output` is `(b, num_heads, q_len, head_dim)`; `transpose(1,2)` makes it
`(b, q_len, num_heads, head_dim)`; line 336's `reshape(bsz, q_len, hidden_size)` flattens
`(num_heads, head_dim)` contiguously. So `o_proj`'s **input** index `i` belongs to head
`i // 128`. Since input index `i` multiplies **column `i`** of a `[out, in]` weight,
**head `h` ↔ columns `[128h : 128h+128)` of `o_proj.weight`**. The spec's
`D[:, h*128:(h+1)*128]` is correct.

**4. GQA mapping — `⌊h / n_rep⌋`, contiguous blocks of 7. CONFIRMED.**
`repeat_kv` (L235–236) inserts a new axis *after* the KV-head axis, expands it to `n_rep`, and
reshapes to `num_key_value_heads * n_rep`. Query index `q` therefore maps to KV head
`q // n_rep`. With `n_rep = 28/4 = 7`: **KV head `g` serves query heads `[7g, 7g+7)`.** The
spec's §5.1 is correct. It is *not* `q % 4`, and it is *not* interleaved. (The docstring at
L229 confirms the intent: "equivalent of `torch.repeat_interleave(x, dim=1, repeats=n_rep)`".)

**5. Rotary does not move head boundaries. CONFIRMED — with one caveat you already flagged.**
`apply_rotary_pos_emb` is called *after* the reshape at L295–297 and operates on the last axis
(`head_dim`) via `rotate_half`, which splits a head's 128 dims at 64 (L178–180). It is a
**within-head** operation and never mixes heads. Two corollaries:
 - Per-head Frobenius energy is safe.
 - The 128 coordinates *inside* a head are not a privileged basis, and HF Qwen2 uses the
   **half-split ("NeoX") convention** — dim `i` is paired with dim `i+64`, *not* `(2i, 2i+1)`.
   So (a) do not do per-dimension attribution inside a head (matching failure mode 2 of the
   spec), and (b) if you ever form a QK circuit `W_q^h R_θ W_k^{g,ᵀ}`, the rotary rotation must
   be inserted with the half-split pairing, or the result is silently wrong.

**6. All attention backends agree.** In 4.46.3 the eager (L295–297 / 335–336), SDPA
(L505–507 / 553–554) and flash-attention (L381–383 / 430–432 / 456) paths use identical head
views and identical final reshapes. The head mapping is **backend-independent**, so Phase 2's
hook on `o_proj`'s input is valid regardless of `attn_implementation`.

**7. Biases are irrelevant at head granularity — and this closes the normalisation error.**
`q/k/v_proj` have biases (L271–273) and `o_proj` does not (L274). Your diff shows all biases
bitwise unchanged, so `Δb ≡ 0` and the per-head **weight** block is the *complete* per-head
change. State this once in E8 and the bias-in-denominator problem cannot recur at head level.

## 6. Weight-space vs activation-space evidence: what a weight-only finding licenses

**It licenses a statement about parameters, not about function.** Three verified anchors:

- **Hase et al., *Does Localization Inform Editing?*** (**arXiv:2301.04213**, NeurIPS 2023
  spotlight) **[V]** — localisation conclusions from causal tracing are *statistically
  uncorrelated* with which layer is best to edit. If a *causal* localisation does not predict
  where an intervention works, a *weight-magnitude* localisation certainly does not.
- **Michel et al. 1905.10650** **[V]** — most heads can be individually removed with no
  significant performance loss. Structural presence ≠ functional necessity; a head with a large
  `ΔW` may be doing nothing.
- **Shuttleworth et al. 2410.21228** **[V]** — the correct template: observe the spectral
  structure, then **causally intervene** on it (rescale the intruder dimensions) before making
  the functional claim.
- **arXiv:2604.08844** **[V]** — geometry-to-behaviour correlation ρ = 0.72 is meaningful but
  far from deterministic, and cross-method generalisation "fails completely".

**What you may write from Phase 1 alone:**
> "The rank-16 attention-only update is distributed across attention heads
> {concentrated in / indistinguishably from} a matched random rank-16 subspace, in both
> organisms, at layers 22–25."

**What you may not write from Phase 1 alone:** any sentence containing "the loyalty lives in
head *h*", "head *h* implements", "head *h* is responsible for", or "these heads carry the
behaviour". The bridge is Phase 2's `Δz_h = ΔW_o a_h^org + W_o^base Δa_h` split — and even that
is *correlational* until you ablate a head block and measure the behavioural swing. Given the
98-percentage-point refusal gap you already have, a per-head ablation is a cheap and decisive
follow-up and should be pre-registered as Phase 3.

**One asymmetry worth exploiting:** weight-only evidence is *strong* for **negative/structural**
claims and *weak* for **positive/functional** ones. "227 of 339 tensors are bitwise identical,
therefore no MLP, no LayerNorm, no embedding and no unembedding was touched" is a *proof*, not
an inference, and it rules out entire hypothesis classes at zero cost. Lead with the structural
exclusions; they are the part weight-space does better than anything else.

## Quantity → definition → sensitivity → caveat

| quantity | definition | sensitive to | reporting caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| `‖D‖_F` (absolute) | `√Σ d_ij²` | overall magnitude **and** tensor size | never compare across shapes |
| `rel_fro` | `‖D‖_F / ‖W_base‖_F`, same tensor | magnitude relative to what was there | **state the denominator**; never pool weight + bias; scale-free only if the base tensor's scale is meaningful |
| per-parameter RMS | `‖D‖_F / √numel` | mean per-entry change | the size-fair scalar; the one to use across differently-shaped tensors |
| energy share | `‖D_g‖_F² / ‖D‖_F²` | group magnitude **and** group size | meaningless without the parameter share; report **enrichment** = share / param-share |
| enrichment | `(energy share)/(param share)` | genuine over/under-representation | uniform = 1.0; state it in the caption |
| support / bit-identity count | `#{tensors with D ≡ 0}` | exact structure | a categorical fact — keep it out of continuous norms; report separately |
| nominal rank | LoRA `r` | the training config | a claim, not a measurement; must be checked |
| numerical rank@τ | `#{σ_i > τ}` | the threshold `τ` | show insensitivity over ≥1 decade of τ; the NumPy/MATLAB default `σ₁·max(m,n)·eps` **degenerates for bf16** (`max(m,n)·eps ≈ 28`) |
| energy rank@α | min `k` with `Σ_{i≤k}σ² ≥ α Σσ²` | the tail of the spectrum | pick α before looking; report 0.90 and 0.99 |
| Roy–Vetterli erank | `exp(−Σ p_i ln p_i)`, `p_i = σ_i/Σσ_j` | the whole spectral shape | **uses σ, not σ²** — say so, or it is unreproducible |
| entropy rank (energy) | same with `p_i = σ_i²/Σσ_j²` | the whole shape, weighted by energy | a *different* number from Roy–Vetterli; never call both "effective rank" |
| stable rank | `Σσ_i² / σ₁²` | dominated by `σ₁` | collapses toward 1 whenever one direction is large; low value ≠ low rank |
| participation ratio | `(Σλ)²/Σλ²`, `λ_h = ‖D_h‖_F²` | concentration of a share vector | equals #groups if uniform; equals 1 if one group carries all; define inline (no canonical ML citation) |
| Gini of shares | standard Gini on `{share_h}` | inequality across the *whole* distribution | more sensitive to the tail than PR; report both, they can disagree |
| `p_max` (top share) | `max_h share_h` | the single largest block only | needs a matched null; uniform baseline = 1/#blocks |
| cosine between task vectors | `⟨τ_a, τ_b⟩/(‖τ_a‖‖τ_b‖)` | direction only | in high dim, random cosine ≈ 0 with tiny variance — always report the random null (E1a already does) |
| subspace cosine / principal angles | cosines of principal angles between column spans | shared directions, ignoring magnitude | random-subspace null is mandatory and dimension-dependent |
| noise plateau level | flat `σ_i/σ₁` for `i > r` | storage precision, matrix size | compare to `2s√n` (RMT bulk edge), **not** to a per-element unit roundoff |
